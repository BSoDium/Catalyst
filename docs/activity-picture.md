# The activity picture (software page)

The 3D picture on `/software`: a year of GitHub contributions (green, on top) and WakaTime coding time (blue, below) as glass blocks, one column a day. Code: the picture itself is the standalone library `neon-activity-graph` (`packages/neon-activity-graph`, MIT, destined for its own repository: its README documents the API, the options, the optics and how to regenerate the caustic templates): `src/graph.ts` (the scene, raw three.js), `ground-light.ts` (the light on the ground), `neon.ts` (the start-up), `view.ts` and `layout.ts` (the geometry shared with the place-holder, and the grid of days), `glass-caustics.ts` (the readable reference of the optics, with the tests). What stays in `apps/web` is the site's own: `components/activity-panel.tsx` (the panel, figures, legend, place-holder), `components/activity-scene.tsx` (the library's React view with the site's theme, background, `?xmas` lights and bells, and the dev helpers), `lib/activity.ts` (the file's shape, the figures, the day's words), `lib/xmas.ts` and `lib/xmas-lights.ts`, `components/sleigh-bells.ts`. The data is `activity.json` on the `data` branch (`.github/workflows/activity.yml`, every 6 hours, `[vercel skip]`).

## How the light is made

- **Light theme**: the glass's reflections (the room's environment and the clear coat: `LIGHT_ENV`, `LIGHT_COAT` in the package's `graph.ts`) are kept fainter than what comes through it, and the specular colour is the glass's own, so the top and the edges read as glass, not as metal. The sun through the glass, traced on the graphics card (the package's `ground-light.ts`, a vertex shader; the same optics as `glass-caustics.ts`): refraction, Fresnel, total internal reflection, the glass's colour absorption, rounded edges. The ground's brightness is a signed difference to what it would have got with no glass, so shadows are coloured and soft and caustics are the surplus.
- **Dark theme**: there is no sun, the light is each block's rod. What one block casts on the ground is **baked**: the package's `assets/caustic-templates.png` (41 KB, embedded as a data URI in `src/templates-data.ts`) holds path-traced templates (Mitsuba 3) of a glass block with a rod in it, for 3 heights x 4 heights above the ground, averaged over the square's symmetries so they are clean. The page lays the right template under every block (`stamps` in `ground-light.ts`), in the rod's colour. Tracing this live was tried and does not work: with the rays the page can afford the result is noise.
- Regenerating the templates (only if the blocks' shape or the rod changes): `packages/neon-activity-graph/scripts/caustic-templates/README.md`.

### Tunables (all in the package's `graph.ts` unless said)

`CAUSTIC_POWER` (dark light strength), `STAMP_CONTRAST` and `STAMP_FOOT` (`ground-light.ts`: how much of the faint wide glow and of the bright foot of a block is kept), `POOL` (the soft haze), `ABSORB`, `STRAIGHT`, `SHADOW`, `GLEAM` (light theme), the bloom's blue/green factors in the show shader, `OFF_EMISSIVE`, `OFF_ENV` and the rod's off colour (the neon's off state).

## The neon start-up (dark theme)

When the picture first appears in the dark theme the rods flicker on like gas tubes: the blocks are split into 8 oval areas (not square), each with its own stutter, same for all its blocks (a few ms and a little brightness off from one another), then a warm-up. A block whose rod is off is dim and the same for all (cool grey), lit by the blocks around it that are on. The ground light follows each rod. Sound is synthesised (Web Audio, no file): a low 100 Hz ballast hum that dies away slowly and short bell-like dings (a third of them low), faint; not every tube dings: each has its own voice, the same every time (about half ding, a third give a faint electrical buzz instead (band-passed noise chopped at 100 Hz, 70-150 ms), the rest are silent); it plays only if the browser lets the page (not before the visitor has interacted with the page). The wobble: every tube's brightness drifts quickly (two sines of its own, 4-8 Hz and 8-16 Hz, redrawn 40 times a second, 15 on a weak device, never faster than 0.4 of that, its own strength: about one tube in five wobbles, the others stay steady) by an amount that follows the hum's loudness (rising as the tubes light, fullest when the start-up has settled, gone with the hum's decay, about 10 s after the start-up; down to 55 % of its brightness for the strongest tube at the peak). No sound of its own; tubes in a flicker are left to it. Every light update is drawn as 2-3 samples at once and shown together, so the picture never shimmers. Once everything is on, at first every 3-20 s, then less and less (hardly ever after a few minutes), a batch of tubes (often a whole segment of up to 18 next to one another, sometimes a few, rarely one) flickers like a sign's: out with a lower ding, sometimes a stutter, back on with a higher one; completely random, only while the picture is in view and the tab visible, nothing changes in the final picture. It is skipped for `prefers-reduced-motion`. It runs on every landing on the dark theme; there is no flag (it is skipped for `prefers-reduced-motion`).

Dev-only helpers (not in the production build): `?lite` (the light version on any machine), `?neonhold` (the picture held half on, half off; it needs the dev server), and the `data-*` figures on the picture's box (`data-frame-ms`, `data-neon-ticks`, `data-neon-apply-ms`, `data-neon-frame-ms`).

## Testing checklist (the neon plays for everyone; run it on the PR preview or the production build)

Measured so far (dev build, Apple M4): an update of the neon costs about 0.05 ms, a frame about 0.5 ms. Emulated Pixel 7 (`activity-neon.mjs`, CPU x6, the Mac's GPU): update 0.7-0.8 ms, frame 4-5 ms, rAF p95 16.8 ms during the start-up; the page's one-time build stalls the main thread for up to 1.3 s at x6 (dev build, cold). None of this is a real phone's GPU.

1. **Emulated, repeatable**: `pnpm --filter @catalyst/web dev`, then `node apps/web/scripts/perf/activity-neon.mjs --device pixel7 --throttle 6` (also `--device mobile`, `--throttle 4` and `--throttle 10`). Look at `rafMs.p95`, `longFrames` and `dataset.neonFrameMs`. Repeat on a **production build** (the dev figures above include Vite's compile; a production build has no `data-*`, so use the rAF figures).
2. **Real devices** (a production build, on the real network: the PR's Vercel preview will do): a recent iPhone (Safari), a mid-range and a low-end Android (Chrome), an older laptop with integrated graphics, a 4K or 5K desktop display. For each: does the start-up run smoothly (no visible stutter beyond the flicker), does the page stay scrollable during it, does the picture end in the same state as with the start-up skipped (reduced motion), does the device warm up or the battery drop noticeably over 5 loads in a row.
3. **Sound**: with sound on and off, silent after a direct reload (blocked by the browser, nothing else breaks), audible after the visitor has clicked; not startling at the system's usual volume; nothing plays when the tab is in the background or after the visitor leaves the page; not played under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
4. **States**: switch light/dark theme during the start-up (it restarts cleanly, no stuck dim blocks); resize and rotate the phone during it; scroll the picture off and back; reduced motion; a page with the data failing to load (no picture, no error).
5. **Both themes, all widths**: the legend and figures at 1300, 1700 and 2560 px; the phone layout (4 strips, bleeding off the edges); the dark glow's brightness against the cubes' (the ground must never be brighter than the blocks).
6. **Open decisions**: the sound has no visible switch (it plays when the browser allows it and is silent otherwise; add a mute control if that is not enough); cap the update rate on `lite` further if a real phone needs it (`fps` in `createNeon`, and `samplesCap` in the package's `graph.ts`), and rerun 1-5 on the production build.

## Year headings on a phone (software and the other lists)

The year heading keeps its big numeral where it started. When it sticks under the bar (`data-stuck`, set by `EntryListPage` from the scroll, since a sticky element cannot be asked) it shrinks, in 0.4 s, a little (to 0.82) to a square-cornered blurred plate with a hairline ring that floats above the rows (`.ds-year-sticky` in `app.css`, below 48 rem). Unstuck it has the room under it that it has on a desktop (a heading, with the first row clear of it). It shrinks with a transform, not a size: a smaller box would pull the rows up and un-stick it.

## Camera grain

A fine, fixed noise over the whole picture, in both themes, added in the last (show) pass after the lighting (`grain` option of the library, default 0.012, the strength settled on by eye on a real screen: 0.004 was barely there, 0.018 started to show as grain; 0 turns it off. To tweak it, change the constant and look at it on a real screen: the browser pane's downscaling misleads). It is meant to read as a camera's, not as rendering noise: soft (the sum of three uniform numbers), the same for every colour, growing with the light (the square root of the brightness, as a sensor's does), eased off in the highlights so that a bright surface does not burn, none in the near-blacks (in 8 bits a tiny noise there only flips pixels between two levels, a speckle), brightness only: it scales the colour by a few per cent (hue kept), never adds a dark speck, and has no colour noise. Static (the picture is drawn once, not every frame).

## The day card on a phone

The card's outer box is zero-sized (its 256 px box, translated to the hovered column, used to widen the page by up to 120 px when a block near the right edge was tapped: the page scrolled sideways and the fixed bar moved off-screen), and the section clips sideways overflow (`overflow-x: clip`, which does not make a scroll container, so the sticky year headings keep working).

## Layout (software page)

- From 48 rem (768 px) the picture is one long strip and the page's and the year's figures are in ONE grid in its top right corner (`Figures` in `activity-panel.tsx`; the head's own figures are hidden then). From 56 rem the legend (the GitHub / WakaTime links) is in the bottom left corner too; below that it is under the picture. The grid and the legend scale with the page (`cqi`).
- Phone: each strip is moved along x so that the weight of its blocks (not only its rectangle) is centred (`stripShifts`, the package's `view.ts`: a shorter last strip and strips whose busy days lie to one side), and the picture is centred on the strips' centre of gravity, not on its bounding box (`fit` in the package's `graph.ts`); its box is pulled up 12 cqi under the figures (they are above it, `z-10`, so no text is covered).
- Below 48 rem (a phone) the picture is four strips, staggered along x by the amount that cancels the camera's tilt (`slabFor` in the package's `view.ts`: the strips are stacked straight on the screen, and the picture is about a third narrower than with the strips side by side), in a box of aspect 0.8; the figures are in the head and under the picture.
- Hovering a day (dark theme): after 70 ms its blocks glow (x1.7) and the others dim (x0.42), eased; `FOCUS_ON` / `FOCUS_OFF` in the package's `graph.ts`. The light theme has none yet.
- Easter egg (`lib/xmas.ts`): `/software?xmas` always shows it, `?xmas=0` never; otherwise it shows in the Christmas season (1 December to 6 January) with a 25 % chance, rolled once per browser session (`XMAS_CHANCE`). The picture, when it shows:  lights every rod in a Christmas-tree colour (red, green, gold, blue, and warm whites, a day always the same one), with the glass left clear and the baked ground light in the rod's colour (dark theme; the library's `rodColor` option, given by `lib/xmas-lights.ts`). Dark theme only (the light theme shows the normal picture); a white "Merry Christmas" in the globe's calligraphy floats over the picture's bottom right (from 56 rem on one line with its baseline measured onto the legend's last line; below that, on two lines, Merry over Christmas, right-aligned and bigger, in its own corner, with no baseline shift and no backdrop below the picture, where the legend is), fades in once the neon start-up is over (`onLit`) and out on hover (its baseline is measured onto the legend's last line). Once, faintly, as the start-up begins (the file is fetched as soon as the picture is built): a recording of sleigh bells ("Bells jingling 2", dragon-studio, trimmed to 3.4 s; `public/audio/sleigh-bells.mp3`, `components/sleigh-bells.ts`, hooked in through the library's `startup` option), only once the browser lets the page play, with the picture in view and the tab visible (retried every second for 20 s, never played twice). Not on by default.
