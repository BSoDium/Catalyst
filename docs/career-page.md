# Career page (`/career`)

The curriculum vitae as a page, for the people who read it (and print it): `apps/web/app/routes/career.tsx`, `components/career-page.tsx`, `lib/career.ts` (dates, languages, structured data: pure), `lib/career-meta.ts` (head tags), styles at the end of `app/app.css` ("The career page"). Data: the optional top-level `career` of the projection ([api-contract.md](api-contract.md#the-career-document-career)); there is no other source and no LLM.

## When it exists

Only while the published projection has a `career` (the owner's private document has `status: published`), or in `pnpm dev` (the preview carries the draft) and on demo content. Without one: `/career` is a real 404 (the site's not-found page), there is no link to it anywhere (header, hero, search), it is not in `sitemap.xml`, and nothing else changes: production has none until the owner publishes. The root loader's `hasCareer` feeds the header and the hero (`lib/use-has-career.ts`); the search index has the row only when the career exists; `scripts/prod/check.mjs` checks the right case from the sitemap (404 and no link, or the page's head, h1, JSON-LD and no contact channel).

## Where it is reached from (owner decisions, 2026-10-14)

- **Home hero:** a fifth quiet row "Career" after Poetry (same row style, arrow, no count; phone too). It is not a content kind, so the caption's section names are unchanged ([landing.md](landing.md)).
- **Header:** a "Career" link after the four sections and before the search. Seven items need tighter tiers (`[data-career]` in app.css); under 24 rem of header (a phone narrower than 384 px) the link is left out there: the hero row and the search have it.
- **Search palette:** one row, type `career` (words: career, CV, resume, experience, education, employers and skills named in it).
- Not a section: no list, no count, not in the sections table (`lib/sections.ts`).

## Head tags and structured data

Title `Career · <name>`, description the profile statement cut at 160 characters, canonical, the site's default `og:image` (the portrait is never the share image and never in the structured data), one JSON-LD `ProfilePage` about a `Person` with only the site's own name and role line, `url` and `sameAs` (the document's public links); no employer, place, image or contact detail. Sitemap: `/career` with `lastmod` = the reviewed month. Robots: as every page (allowed on the production host, disallowed elsewhere).

## The portrait's Easter egg (owner, 2026-11-03)

Now and then the portrait crashes: its face becomes a Windows blue screen of death (the owner's own picture, a screenshot masked to the face's shape, with the sad face and the QR codes). `career.portrait.egg` `{ src, width, height, dark? }` is that picture (the same blueprint, its own dark variant: the frame's ground is part of the file). Every load of `/career` rolls `PORTRAIT_EGG.chance` (3 %, `lib/portrait-egg.ts`, in the browser only: the server's page is always the plain portrait, so it is the same for every visitor and the cache); when it hits, the egg's file is loaded, and 1.4 s after the page (the plain portrait is seen first) the picture is replaced, with a quick run of stepped flickers (`portrait-crash`, 0.3 s, not under reduced motion): the egg is laid OVER the plain portrait, which it matches everywhere but on the face, so only the face flickers (the bleed behind is swapped at once). Its alt text is a fixed line (it is a joke, not the owner's description). **To see it: `/career?bsod`** (`?bsod=0` never). Without an `egg` in the document nothing happens.

The drawings: `docs/portrait/avatar.svg` is the owner's drawing (the one of the Avatar repo, 500 x 500), `build-blueprint.mjs avatar.svg portrait.svg` redraws it as the blueprint (a seeded pen: the same drawing every run; the glasses and the single face-and-neck shape of the new drawing), `... portrait-bsod.svg --bsod` the egg's twin (it puts `bsod-screen.part.svg`, the owner's picture, over the skin), `render.mjs` renders either to a PNG in a scheme at 2x (1880 x 1280, the size of the site's files) and the `.webp` files here are those renders (`portrait`, `portrait-dark`, `portrait-bsod`, `portrait-bsod-dark`), which go to the private content repo as the portrait's files. The demo has the SVGs (`apps/web/public/media/demo/portrait-example*.svg`).

## The projects of a position (owner, 2026-11-03, #531)

A position may list the projects worked on during it (`roles[].projects`, [api-contract.md](api-contract.md)): work under a contract has no repository to link to, so a recruiter gets the context here, as on LinkedIn. They are **a row of their own under the head and the detail, across the whole width of the item** (owner: a column at the left made the left side tall and the right one short): from 64 rem in the item's own two columns (a project at the right starts where the highlights do), under 64 rem one after the other, after the skills. A "Selected work" label in the mono face (the site calls its own section Software, and a test keeps the word "Projects" out of its labels), then each project as **a picture and a text block of the same height, a bit like LinkedIn does** (owner): a 10 rem picture (8 rem on a phone; a 16:9 rectangle on a desktop, it follows the text block's height, never under 5.6 rem, cropped from the top left, one hairline frame), and at its right the title in medium weight, cut to two lines, and the line in the reading face, muted, **cut to two with an ellipsis** where it would overflow. **The project is a link to the site's own entry for it** (`entry`, a software entry of the projection: the full text, the picture and the facts are there; the contract work that has no repository of its own gets an entry like the rest), the whole of it clickable (the title underlines on hover); without an entry the site has, it is only the card. **No dividers between them** (owner: "don't overuse dividers"): the spacing does it. Print shows the whole text. Without projects nothing is drawn. The demo has two positions with projects (one with a picture and an entry, one with a picture only, one plain).
