# Landing hero

The home page (`/`): the pixel globe with a typographic hero over it. Design source: the owner's canvas boards "home-rest" and "home-touched" (2026-10-12; 1440 x 900, dark scheme; both schemes are built from the app tokens, nothing is copied from the boards' hex values). All type, no boxes, no ornament; the accent appears once (a row or header item under the pointer or the keyboard). Identity change (2026-10-13): the big type is the owner's NAME, not the project's code name, and the four rows replace the header's section links on this page.

Round 3 (2026-10-13, owner feedback items 3 and 10; reworked 2026-10-14 after the owner's review): the home page opens fully zoomed OUT and, at rest, shows the planet with the flat **ring of type** as it always was (zoomed out) and a **caption** wrapped on a sphere (round 3 drew it in the globe's own pixel pass; since round 4 it is crisp, in a second canvas), instead of boxes and labels; the caption replaces the how-to line; the planet is **lifted** clear of the name; and the collapse **shrinks the name** into the corner, the name itself being what stays there. See "Showcase" below.

Code: `app/components/landing-hero.tsx` (the markup and the name's travel), `app/lib/hero-machine.ts` (the state machine and `showcaseOf`, pure), `app/hooks/use-landing-hero.ts` (wiring to the shell: the machine, the showcase signal and the measured cover), `app/routes/shell.tsx` (mounts it), the "Landing hero" block at the end of `app/app.css`. Check: `scripts/home/hero.mjs`; the globe's side: `scripts/globe/ring.mjs`.

```
    button.hero-search  [data-needs-script] the search icon (control size), "Search places and entries" (mono 13), the key hint (command icon + K, or Ctrl K); opens the existing palette

```

Weights: the given name is the heavy half (800, set at -0.02 em: at the name's -0.055 em the three l, l, i of "Elliot" touched), the family name the thin half (200, -0.055 em). The hyphen of the family name has its own span (`.hero-hyphen`, 0.045 em of margin). Gutters 56 px (`clamp(1rem, 3.9vw, 3.5rem)`). Under 48 rem it stacks in two lines: the given name over the family name, whose width is what fills the row there (`--hero-fit` 4.7, gutter to gutter: 76 px at 390), then intro, hint, search, rows. Wide and short (under 34 rem tall, from 48 rem wide): the rows in two columns, the name capped at 26 vh and the hint dropped (the globe is in plain view; the line saved is room for it).

**The whole block is always in the window** (review of 2026-10-13: at 667 x 375, 720 x 450 = a 1440 x 900 window at 200 % zoom, 320 x 568 and 320 x 480 the stacked block was 510 to 540 px tall, bottom-anchored in an `overflow: hidden` main, so the name was at negative y). From 320 x 480 and from 568 x 320 up:
- The stacked name's size is also what the height leaves (`--hero-size` takes `(100dvh - --hero-fixed - --hero-clear - safe area - 1rem) / 1.9`; `--hero-fixed` is what the block holds besides the name, per tier: 26.5 rem, 24.5 rem without the hint, 17 rem with the rows in two columns, 13.5 rem without the second sentence), never over the width fit and 24 vh, never under 1.75 rem.
- Tiers under 48 rem: up to 40 rem tall the how-to line is gone; up to 36 rem the four rows are two columns of 44 px targets; under 30 rem wide and up to 28 rem tall only who the owner is stays of the intro (the second sentence is its own span, `.hero-intro-more`).
- A phone on its side, 30 to 48 rem wide and up to 34 rem tall: the wide layout in small (intro and search left, the two-by-two rows right, the name on ONE line across the bottom at 72 % of the width, 26 vh at most); at 22 rem tall, or 28 rem under 40 rem wide, also without the second sentence.
- The dev badge raises the block by 4.75 rem under 48 rem (`--hero-clear`): the tiers hold with it. Below 320 x 480 (320 x 320 and the like, which no phone has) the block can still overflow by up to 25 px: not supported.
- The sizes are measured in `scripts/home/hero.mjs` (15 shapes, light and dark, top and bottom of the block's box against the window, the globe reachable above it). DOM order is reading order (name, intro, search, rows); the grid places the name last on a wide screen.

The block is bottom-anchored and rises by the credits line's height plus what the "y" hangs under the line box (`padding-bottom: clear + 0.15 * name size`), so no letter is ever over the credits line (bottom right, 8 to 30 px from the bottom) or, in development, the content badge. The hero is `pointer-events: none`; only the search line and the rows take the pointer, so the globe stays draggable and zoomable beneath all the rest. A page-colour fade (`.landing-hero::before`, no blur) sits behind the thin type.

## Showcase (round 3)

While the hero is `rest` at the world view (`showcaseOf(snapshot, cover)`: `state === "rest" && world`) the home screen is a **showcase**: nothing else of the globe's overlay competes with the caption.

- **Start zoomed out.** The page opens at the globe's lowest zoom (`GlobeRenderer.startView`: `minZoom`, the whole-globe fit minus the room the ring needs, 0.5 levels at every checked viewport) instead of the fit; the flat ring of type (unchanged, text from `SITE`) is shown from the first frame. `GlobeViewState.zoom` still reports 0 below the fit. A debug page (`?globe-debug`) keeps the old start unless `?showcase`.
- **What changes.** The boxes and labels fade out (`LodTree.setSuppressed`: every node's target goes OFF and runs by its own timed opacity, `FADE_MS` 200 ms, no re-planning) while the caption's lines come round the planet from behind (`docs/web-architecture.md` "Caption on the sphere": 900 ms ease-out-expo, each row 450 ms after the one before, each from a random side; on load and every time the showcase returns). The hidden boxes are inert (a node that is off takes no pick, and `pickAt` answers null while the showcase is on). The first gesture (a press or a wheel over the globe: the engine's own capture listener, and the hero's `onInteract`) ends it at once: the boxes come back, the lines go on behind the planet in 200 ms and the press that began it selects nothing. The places list, the keyboard and the URL paths are untouched (a place opening takes the hero to `collapsed` and ends it).
- **The planet is lifted.** While the showcase is on the planet's centre goes UP by what makes its lowest point clear the name (the hero measures `name.offsetTop` from the page's bottom: `LandingHero` `onCover`, a `ResizeObserver`, layout offsets so the name's own transform never changes it) plus 14 px, as far as the room above allows (`liftFor`, `engine/lift.ts`: the highest thing, the planet or the ring's top, keeps 14 px from the top edge). It follows the cover when it changes, tweened like the panel's inset (`TUNING.insetMs`, `INSET_EASE`), and comes down in the same time when the showcase ends. Reduced motion: at once. The lowest zoom, the fit and the framing clamps use the same centre (`liftBuf` in every projection: the camera's view offset, the markers, the label tree's `centreY`, the sky's disc, the ring's centre).
- **Words.** The ring is the identity line unchanged (`ringPhrases(SITE)`). The caption is `siteCaption(counts)` in `lib/site.ts`, English, FOUR rows in view, never five (a fifth, when photography became a section, made the block too heavy: owner, 2026-10-26): the call to action "Drag to explore." / "Pick a place." stays at the bottom (the same size as every row) and the two rows above it take turns every 5.5 s: "A personal archive," / "organised around places." (all of them drawn in capitals, the page's text is as written; the rows are on the equator, so the camera is held inside a latitude band while the caption shows: a return after a tilt first glides the latitude, then the entrance); after it comes in each row ROLLS slowly through the centre (8 degrees either side over the 5.5 s dwell, in the direction it came in from), then accelerates out and the next phrase set comes in the same way; the five sections as one sentence in two rows, "Poems, software, art," / "articles & photography." (each word in its section's face: "software" in the code face, "Poems" in the calligraphic script, capitalised as the page spells it and with no descender to meet the row under it, 1.5 times the serif's size and on the TOP row: nothing is above it there, so it rises clear of any row, and its baseline is the serif's: `CAPTION_FACES.script.scale`; the widest row is 24 letters, no wider than the counts' row was; the sections' own names are the hero's rows and the header's links, as text); the count from the projection ("64 entries," / "in 5 sections.", left out when unknown). Interface copy and facts, no code name can reach it. Set in Newsreader (the intro's face) with a few heavier words: text-coloured letters (the secondary text tone; two words at most in BSoD Blue) in a page-coloured contour and thickness that cut the globe's lines round each glyph. The sets stop turning while the user interacts, while the tab is hidden, when the hero is collapsed or away, and under reduced motion (set 1, static); the page's hidden how-to line and the rows are the complete static copy.
- **Leaving.** A gesture ends the showcase: the caption FADES (180 ms, only its opacity) and stays locked to the globe while it does (zoom, pinch, drag); the boxes and labels come back and the flat ring leaves with them, in the same frames and on the same clock (it is back with the caption when the hero returns to rest). Closing a panel or collapsing the hero never flashes either.
- **Accessibility.** The caption is pixels in the globe's canvas (not in the accessibility tree, not selectable). The how-to line is visually hidden (not removed) while the caption is there; without scripts (`scripting: none`), without WebGL, with `?no-ring` and on the empty archive (`data-orbit="false"`) it shows as before.
- **Off switches.** `?no-ring` (the ring AND the caption, the lowest zoom below the fit, the showcase): the page is the previous one, the boxes stay and the hint shows.

## Room for the globe (phones and tablets, 2026-10-20)

The caption (the 3D words) must never run over a line of the hero, and the globe should be as big as that allows. The hero reports the blocks of the home screen over the globe (`GlobeShowcase.blocks`: the name, the intro and search, the list; their span across the container and the y of their top, measured by `LandingHero`; the name from layout offsets, so its own travel never moves it). The globe works out its size and place itself (`engine/lift.ts` `placeAbove`, used by `GlobeRenderer.lowestFor`):

- **The caption's lowest line sits `LIFT.gapPx` above the highest block under the column the words span** (`CAPTION_HALF_R`, 1.04 radii to each side of the centre; the lowest line is `CAPTION_BOTTOM_R`, 0.32 radii below the centre: measured on the page). The planet's own lower part may go under the content: it is a drawing that fades into the page behind the hero's scrim; only the words must be readable.
- **That is the minimum; more room is used.** The planet may be as big as its whole ring fits the window (`ringRoomRadius`, no longer capped half a level under the whole-globe fit), and between the highest and the lowest place it can take, the globe sits in the MIDDLE of the free room: the space above its ring equal to the space between its bottom and what stands under it (the whole planet clear of the content when there is room for that).
- **The biggest planet that does it**: searched from the largest the window allows downward, since a smaller planet has a narrower column and can clear a block a larger one would stand over (a wide window keeps its big planet between the text at its sides; on a tablet the list rises into the column). The ring's top stays `LIFT.marginPx` inside the window, and the centre is as low as the middle of the page allows (never lower: the globe is not pushed down).
- **The lowest zoom is that size** (`minZoom`), the lift is the centre's rise, and a view resting at the old floor follows it when the blocks change.
- **The ring (flat type round the planet) is cut where the content begins** (`RingLayer.setCut`: a mask from the highest block's top under its column), so no ring letter runs over the text; the planet and the caption are not cut.
- **No letters that would be cut**: when the planet at that zoom is under 60 px of radius (`MIN_TEXT_PLANET_PX`), neither ring nor caption is drawn (their letters have a smallest size); the planet is still there.
- A phone upright holds the rows in two columns (five rows are three lines), and a short one drops the second sentence of the intro, so the globe has more of the page.
- A page that gives no blocks (only `coverBottom`) keeps the earlier lift (`liftFor`: the planet and its ring clear of one cover height).

Checked in the browser pane at 375 x 812, 320 x 568, 812 x 375, 850 x 1043, 768 x 1024 and 2000 x 1158. Not checked: a real phone, Safari, a tablet on its side.

**The planet comes closer when it is taken (phones, 2026-10-29).** The home screen's planet is small on a phone (a fourth of the screen), and once the visitor takes the globe that feels distant. On the first gesture on it the planet glides (`IMMERSION.glideMs`) to the phone's WORLD VIEW (`engine/immersion.ts`): a radius of `IMMERSION.overscan` half widths (at most `IMMERSION.maxHeightShare` of the height), so the disc is wider than the screen and fills over half its height, the sides cut off. The strength follows the window's width (all of it up to `LANDING.narrowPx`, none from `LANDING.widePx`), so a tablet gets most of it and a desktop none. Everything the whole-globe fit is for the world view is measured from that zoom (`GlobeRenderer.world`): the night, the sun and its flare, the moon, the idle rotation and the way back to the home screen. A pan does not end the glide; a zoom of the visitor's does. `GlobeViewState.zoom` is still 0 at the fit. The home screen's way back is measured from the same world view: the hero machine counts a view as the world view by `zoom` (0 at the fit) OR by the renderer's word, `GlobeViewState.world`, present only on a narrow screen where the world view is closer than the fit; without it the idle return never fired from the closer planet.

## States

| State | When | What shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rest` | load; or idle at the world view after a gesture | the whole hero |
| `collapsed` | after a gesture on the globe; or whenever the view is not the world view (zoomed in, a place open) | the small line bottom-left (left 40 px, bottom 32 px: the name in Bricolage 30: "Elliot" at 800 and "Négrel-Jerzy" at 200, as in the hero, and the hint). The h1 stays in the accessibility tree at opacity 0; the search line and the rows are `visibility: hidden` (not tab stops). The hint is shown only while the way back is real (`data-world="true"`) |
| `away` | a place or an entry is open | nothing is shown or exposed (instant); the name renders as a `p`, so the panel's title is the only h1 |

**Closing a panel never flashes the hero (2026-10-14 fix).** `away` hides only the hero's container (`visibility: hidden`, no transition), so every part inside must already wear the COLLAPSED look while away: the intro, the how-to line, the search line, the rows and the plate's `::before` are keyed on `[data-hero="collapsed"], [data-hero="away"]` in app.css. Before, they kept their resting styles under `away` and the way back to `collapsed` faded them out in the open (the intro appeared and faded). The machine's side is a test: from `away` the state goes straight to `collapsed` when the globe is zoomed in on the place, and to `rest` only at the world view after the normal idle delay (`hero-machine.test.ts`, "a place or an entry opens and closes"); `landing-hero.test.ts` holds the selectors; `hero.mjs` samples the intro, hint, rows and search line every frame for a second after Escape from a place and fails above 5 % opacity (it read 1.0 before the fix).

Transitions: opacity and transform only (visibility follows the fade), `--duration-base` (220 ms) with `--ease-standard`; `prefers-reduced-motion`: none (instant). No JS animation loop; a state change costs one React re-render of the hero, and only on a gesture start, the world view being left or reached, or the idle timer.

**The name's travel (owner, items 10 and 3).** On `rest` -> `collapsed` the big name's two halves (`.hero-given`, `.hero-family`, `inline-block`, `transform-origin: 0 0`) shrink and travel to the corner (the ghost line's boxes, `.hero-mini-given` and `.hero-mini-family`), and `collapsed` -> `rest` is the same motion reversed. The halves ARE the collapsed name: there is no second element to hand over to. A FLIP done once at the state change (`travelName` in `landing-hero.tsx`, in a layout effect, before paint): the two text boxes of each half are measured (`Range.getBoundingClientRect`), the scale is the ratio of their widths (ONE uniform scale per half: the ghost is the same drawing at another size, its optical size pinned at the big name's) and the translation puts the box's corner on the other's; the final transform is set as the half's own style at the start (a travel asked for while one is running, collapse then restore within the 320 ms, starts from where the half is: `lib/name-travel.ts`, tested as behaviour) and `Element.animate` runs two `transform` keyframes from identity to it at `NAME_TRAVEL_MS` 320 ms (`--hero-travel`) with `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.9, 0.1, 1)` (a fast start, a soft landing) on the compositor: no font size, no layout, no per-frame JS, no `will-change` outside the animation, and when it ends the animation is cancelled and the same inline transform is the same picture (no frame between). Back: the inline transform is removed at the start and the animation runs from it. While it is held, a resize or a font arriving re-measures it (`ResizeObserver`). The ghost line is `--hero-mini-size` = `clamp(1.75rem, 9.5vw, 2.75rem)`: 44 px at 1440 px, 37 px at 390 px, 30 px at 320 px (it was 30 px, a scale of 0.2; it is 0.28 now), so the corner name clears the credits line, the dev badge and the hint beside it. The rest of the block (intro, hint) fades and slides as before; the hint of the corner line fades in over the last 90 ms. Reduced motion: no animation (the CSS is instant, as before) and the name is held at once. The h1 stays in the accessibility tree and on screen (it is the visible name now), the focus hold, the tab order and the server markup are unchanged; `hero.mjs` checks the hydration warnings.

The machine (`createHeroMachine`): `rest` goes to `collapsed` on `interact()` or when `setWorld(false)`; `collapsed` goes to `rest` after `HERO_IDLE_MS` (8000, the same delay as the globe's idle rotation) without an input, only while the world view holds. Every input restarts the clock; leaving the world view stops it and coming back restarts it. At most one timer, none at rest. **The clock is held while the keyboard focus is in the header (`[data-slot="site-header"]`) or in an open dialog (the search palette)**: the way back removes the header (the rows are the sections then), so a hero that came back under a focused header link, or under the palette whose opener is the header's trigger, left the focus on `body`. `HeroMachine.hold(held)`, fed by `watchFocusHold` (`hooks/use-landing-hero.ts`: `focusin` and `focusout` on the document); releasing starts a whole idle period. "World view": `GlobeViewState.zoom <= 0.03` and no street scale (`isWorldView`; the idle rotation's own slack, 0.15 zoom levels of a range of about 5.3), with no place or entry open.

## Hooks into the globe

- **The showcase** (round 3): `GlobeProps.showcase`, a signal (`{ get(), subscribe() }`, `useLandingHero().showcase`) over the machine and the measured cover: `{ on, coverBottom }`. The globe subscribes, so a gesture or the idle period moves the globe's state without the shell or the globe's props rendering. `GlobeProps.captionPhrases` are the caption's words, `GlobeProps.onOrbit(available)` tells the hero whether the caption exists (it hides its own how-to line only then). Engine side: `GlobeRenderer.setShowcase`, `setCover`, `LodTree.setSuppressed`.
- **A gesture**: `GlobeProps.onInteract` (`globe/types.ts`), implemented in `globe/globe-canvas.tsx`: two passive capture listeners (`pointerdown`, `wheel`) on the globe's own stage, which holds the canvas, so pointer presses (drag, tap on a place, a pinch's fingers) and wheel (including the trackpad pinch) count, hovering does not, the app's overlays and the credits control are outside it, the globe's own motion (rotation, flights, inertia) is not input. The globe has no keyboard zoom (the canvas is not focusable, the places list is the keyboard path), so there is none to hook. No engine file changed.
- **The view**: the shell already receives every reported view (`onViewChange`); `useLandingHero().onView` compares it with the world view and tells the machine only when that changes.
- **The search**: `openSearch()` (`components/search-palette.tsx`) dispatches a window event the palette (mounted once by the navigation) listens to. The hero's line is a `button` (`aria-haspopup="dialog"`, `aria-keyshortcuts`), not a second search; Cmd/Ctrl-K is unchanged.

## The sun

**The real day and night (owner, 2026-10-29).** The page opens with the planet's day and night as they are now: `engine/daylight.ts` finds the subsolar point from the clock, the camera opens over the longitude that puts it `MOON.sunAzimuthDeg` to its right, and the earth rotation angle is set so the camera is still at `MOON.ref` in the inertial frame (the moon's orbit and `litTurn` are as they were). The sun's elevation is the real declination. A debug page (`?globe-debug`) keeps the fixed planet unless `?live-sun` is there.

The light that gives the planet its day and night and the moon its phase (`sunDirection`) is drawn, and it is a point on the skybox (owner, 2026-10-26: "until now we were only simulating it"; the first version was a 2D sprite put where the light falls on the screen, and was rejected: "the sun should be in 3D space, a point on the skybox, as far as the skybox is"). `engine/sun-layer.ts` (`SunLayer`, owned by `GlobeScene` with the moon's flag, `renderOrder` 0.65): the sun is the direction of the light in the earth's frame (`toEarthFrame`), at infinity, projected by the camera as a direction (`w` is the depth along the view) and drawn where the stars are, at the far plane with the depth test and no depth write: the planet hides it where they meet, it is in the picture only where the camera looks towards it, and it turns with the sky as the globe is turned (the camera's own turn: no motion of its own). Its disc is a fixed angle (`SUN.angularDeg`), so it grows with the camera's focal length. The light comes from behind the opening camera, so the sun is not in the picture at the opening view: turn the globe to its far side and it comes round the planet's limb (the planet is then backlit: mostly its night side faces the viewer). It is shown with the planet's night (the same amount: the zoom takes both away, and pages without the showcase have neither; the interlude keeps both, at their full amount).

One quad and one cheap fragment shader, in whole art pixels: a flat disc (0.6 degrees, `SUN.angularDeg`), a big halo round it (`SUN.halo`, 14 radii) that starts strong at the disc's edge (`SUN.dark.glow`, nearly the disc's own tone) and falls steeply (`SUN.haloFall`) into a long tail, in six flat steps of tone dithered into each other (`SUN.haloSteps`: a pixel is one of the steps' tones, never between), and on it a RING of light (`SUN.ring`, owner, 2026-10-27, from a reference photo): sharp on its inside, a smooth falloff on its outside, a little brighter than the halo under it. Static: it asks for no frames of its own. (The versions before this one had a disc with dark spots and a crown of rays, scalloped and then thin and random; the owner, 2026-10-27: "keep it simple, just a circle, a bit smaller, and a very subtle halo".)

- **Dark page:** the brightest thing there is, the ink's own tone, a step down on its limb; the halo barely there (`SUN.dark`).
- **Light page (owner, 2026-10-27: "we never made the colour changes for the light theme", then "much whiter", "the halo very faintly tinted", "the rays white", "much more subtle"):** the sun is burnt out, so it is WHITE (the blur is white laid over the paper and over the grey round it), its rays are white, and what makes them show is not colour but a little darkness: a very soft, faint grey VEIL round the sun (`SUN.light.veil`, in the lens flare's quad: a radial grey, gone with the sun's cover as the flare is), and the halo is that grey too with only `SUN.light.halo.warm` of the warm ramp in it. The ghosts are the ramp's colours at `SUN.light.ghost` strength; the lining is as WHITE as the sun (a hint of the ramp, `SUN.light.lining`) and is drawn OVER the veil (`renderOrder` 0.95 against the flare's 0.9: owner, "the grey behind the sun should make it visible if it is as bright as the sun"; the veil had been painting over it), the ground's light a grey with a trace of the ramp; the twilight is `SUN.light.twilight` of what it is on the dark page. While the planet covers the sun on the light page it does NOT dim (owner: "only shrink it as the crescent shrinks, not opacity"): the blob stays whole white, its size is the crescent's, and the rays keep their strength until the sun is gone; the veil follows the halo's slow fade. (Owner, 2026-10-28, revised: the light page's veil, ghosts and rays now all fade smoothly with the sun's visible radius, the root of its area, down to nothing with its last pixel: they used to hold whole and cut, a jump from dimmed to clear.) The dark page is untouched by all of it.
- **The sunset (owner, 2026-10-27, from a pixel-art reference):** the dark page's light is warm: the halo, the ring and the flare take their colour from one ramp (`SUN.tint`, `WARM_GLSL` in `engine/sun-flare.ts`: pale gold at the top, through orange and red to violet at the tail), per step of tone, and the disc stays the ink's own. The HALO goes with the planet's cover, slowly (`haloClear`, `SUN.fade`: whole while the sun is a good way outside the limb, gone a disc radius behind it, a long smoothstep, not the hair the ring's `planetClear` is), in the halo's own dither, and as it goes the tones are pulled down the ramp (`SUN.setShare`), so the light reddens as it sets. The light page is unchanged: its sun is burnt out.
- **The rim and the silver lining (owner, 2026-10-27; `RIM`, `rimMaterial` in `engine/materials.ts`):** the interlude's atmosphere as a thin skin on the limb, there while the night is (the interlude's own glow takes over from it). Two things: a HAZE, a smooth film of very low opacity (`RIM.haze`) in a soft grey close to the page's colour, on the sun's side of the limb (owner: "grains of sand hovering above the surface can't work", so it is not dithered), and the SILVER LINING, the sun's light scattered by the atmosphere: it is there only while the sun is aligned with the limb on the picture (`RIM.align`: the sun's angle from the view's axis near the planet's angular radius) and only on the part of the outline that faces the sun (`RIM.arc`), in the sunset's colour as a gradient along the limb (the sun's pale gold nearest it, to red and violet away from it), its density AND its opacity rising smoothly (`RIM.grain`: the dither's gaps keep a share of the opacity, so it is not a harsh grain). The same light lies on the GROUND too (`RIM.ground`, `rimGroundMaterial`: owner, 2026-10-27, from NASA footage: "it also illuminates parts of the surface, which gradually becomes dimmer before the edge does"): a warm glow on the surface that is brightest at the limb on the sun's side and dims inward, in the dark side's own dithered levels (`RIM.ground.steps`: owner: "textured, not a smooth gradient"), and only while the sun is above the horizon: it is zero by the time the sun's centre is on the limb (`rimLit`, `RIM.ground.peak`), so a set sun lights only the atmosphere. The sun's halo and ring are NOT behind it any more (owner, 2026-10-28): they are a lens's, drawn over the whole planet like the blob (`renderOrder` 5.15, no depth test, premultiplied, translucent: `SUN.haloOpacity`, unlike the sun's disc, which is opaque).
- **The iris (owner, 2026-10-28; `SUN.light.iris`, `Renderer.stepIris`):** on the light page the backdrop dims a hair (`dim`, a share of its tone) while the sun is in the picture, easing in over `closeMs` as it comes in and out over `openMs` after it leaves, like a diaphragm adapting to the light (time-based, not tied to the camera). The dark page is untouched.
- **The sun is a blur, and a crescent as it sets (owner, 2026-10-27):** on the dark page there is no disc edge: the sun is a blob (`SUN.core`: a gaussian, half as bright at `half` 1 disc radius, the size the old disc was, and nothing by `radius` 2.4; no flat plateau: owner, 2026-10-27, "it's octagonal") in its own layer OVER the planet (`SunLayer.blob`, `renderOrder` 5.2, no depth test: a lens's bloom is not hidden by the limb) that ADDS its light (additive blending: owner, "it has non-1 opacity when it overlaps the earth, which looks odd"; a bright centre is solid over the planet, a dim part is a glow). While the planet covers the sun, the blob is what the eye makes of the crescent that is left (`sunCrescent`, `engine/sun-flare.ts`, the limb taken as a straight line across the disc): it does not shrink to fit the visible side, it overlaps the planet, centred on the crescent's centre of mass (on the line from the planet's centre through the sun), as wide as the crescent (the sun's own width until the limb has reached the disc's centre, then the crescent's), and as bright as the share of the disc's area still in view. The lens flare comes out of the same point at the same strength. Nothing dims before the first pixel of the sun is covered: the halo, the ring and the warm light's reddening go over the sunset only (`SUN.fade`: from the disc's first contact with the limb to the sun's centre 3 radii behind it: the halo is wide, so most of it is still in view when the last pixel of the sun has gone). The light page keeps its ghost of a rim.
- **The lens flare (owner, 2026-10-27; `engine/sun-flare.ts`, `FLARE`):** drawn over everything, as it is in the lens, while the sun is in the picture. Eight one-pixel rays out of the sun (the four axes and the four diagonals, in the sun's own tone, from the disc's edge, thinning out by a Bayer dither), and thirteen low-tone ghosts on the line from the sun through the middle of the picture (hexagons, the shape of an aperture: rings, dithered fills and flat ones, overlapping, each at a share of the way along the line, so they slide, scale and come together as the camera turns). It fades towards the picture's edge, as the sun comes to the middle, and as the planet covers the sun (`planetClear`, the same way the ring of light goes); on the light page it is a faint ghost (`FLARE.lightShare`). Static, like the sun.
- **The moon on the light page:** its lit side is overexposed towards the page's colour (`MOON.burn`, 55 %: not all the way, so that its details are still there) and its dark side has the ground's full tones (`uFlip`, `engine/moon-layer.ts`).

## The moon (#312)

**On a touch screen (2026-10-29).** There is no hover to read the card with, and a moon that goes on moving is not a target, so: once the box is up the moon stands still (`TOUCH_HOLD_MS`, 8 s; if it is not tapped it goes on), the card keeps its two lines (the name over the description, the arrow where it is on a desktop: owner, 2026-10-29, the single-line pill was not liked) but says the second in fewer words (`ARCHIVED_V1.shortDescription`) and is no wider than 17 rem and is on one line, where the full sentence ran across the whole screen, the first tap on it reads it, the second follows the link, and a press anywhere else puts it away. A device with `(hover: none)`; a mouse is as before.

An Easter egg. A small moon, **a lit sphere a sixth of the earth's radius, placed in the globe's own 3D space** (its position, size, light and phase come from the same camera and a real orbit), on a FAR orbit about the planet, drifts through the background now and then and is a link to the first version of the site, which stays as it was: a way back to look at, not a warning. A visitor is meant to miss it the first time: it is not in the picture when the page opens, it is small (about a sixteenth of the planet's size on the screen, as a sixth at three times the distance is), and it is only ever beside the planet, for a few seconds of a four-minute lap, and only while the page is being played with (the idle hold stops it with everything else). Hover or focus it and it holds still, is picked out in the ink by its detection box (the look of the map's area boxes) and a card hung from the box's top-left corner says what it is ("Memory lane", "The first version of this site, kept just as it was.", "Take a trip back ↗"): the label and the description are only there on hover; a click opens `ARCHIVED_V1.url` in a new tab. The address and the words are `ARCHIVED_V1` in `lib/site.ts` (today the old app's own project, `https://v1.bsodium.fr`, see owner-guide.md): the one place to change if the archive moves. The search palette has a "Memory lane" entry (found by "moon", "v1", "version", "old", "first", "archive") for anyone who wants it by name.

Code: `globe/engine/moon.ts` (the orbit, frames, the sun: pure, `moon.test.ts`), `globe/engine/moon-layer.ts` (the sphere and its shader, owned by `GlobeScene`), `globe/engine/moon-overlay.ts` (the link and the card), `GlobeRenderer.syncMoon` / `moonFrame` (the clock, the camera's range, the frames it asks for), the "moon" block of `app.css`; the app hands the words in as `GlobeProps.moon` (`routes/shell.tsx`). Check: `scripts/home/moon.mjs`. The whole 3D picture of the globe, with the moon in it: [globe-3d-report.md](globe-3d-report.md).

- **The orbit.** Radius `MOON.orbit` 9 earth radii, in the INERTIAL frame (the stars' own: the idle rotation leaves it where it is; the scene's earth-fixed position is the inertial one with the longitude taken down by ERA, `toEarthFrame`), one lap in 240 s. That is wider than the camera's own distance from the planet (4 to 9, by window), so the half of the orbit on the camera's side is BEHIND the camera and never seen, and the other half is far in the background, where a sixth of the earth's radius is a small disc (`radius / depth`: 6 % of the planet's on a desktop). It is not the real distance (60 radii is a speck nobody could click). The plane is laid against the camera the page opens with (`MOON.ref`: lon 0 plus ERA 70): inclined 12 degrees from edge-on (10 on a narrow window: `inclinationFor`) so the far half leans up, and rolled 9 degrees on a wide window and level on a narrow one (`rollFor`). Seen from the opening view the moon comes round the planet's LEFT a little above its middle, goes behind the planet (hidden by the depth buffer, for about a third of the lap), and comes out on the right and leaves; the planet fills the picture's height (its top is a few tens of px from the window's), so the sky is at its sides, not above it. It starts at 100 degrees (behind the camera), so a fresh page has no moon in it. A visitor who turns the globe sees the same orbit from another side, as one would.
- **A mesh in the globe's own canvas, in whole pixels.** (The versions before this one: a flat disc; a mesh on the art grid; a separate sprite moved in device pixels; a mesh drawn with COVERAGE, a soft edge and smooth light, which the owner found "heavily anti-aliased", 2026-10-26.) The moon is a sphere in the globe's scene (`moon-layer.ts`, `renderOrder` 0.7: after the planet's disc, which writes the depth that hides it, and after the sky; before the lines, which its depth hides in turn), so it is on the SAME art grid as the planet and the planet hides it by the depth buffer, exactly, at its own stepped limb. Its pixels are whole, like everything else on the globe: a pixel is in or out of the silhouette (a sphere's silhouette is where the view ray is at `asin(r / D)` from the line to its centre, one angle, exact off the axis), the light is three flat grey steps, the ground three hard tones with the Earth's cuts, the rim a solid band. The orbit's slowness keeps the motion even, as the planet's turn is. The ring of type (a 2D canvas after the globe's) and the caption's canvas are over it, so the ring's letters are never hidden by the moon.
- **Frames.** Coverage only glides if there are frames to show it in. While the moon is in the picture and may move it asks for the DISPLAY's own frames (`GlobeRenderer.wantsDisplayRate`, not the idle rotation's ten a second: a few seconds of each lap), and none otherwise. (The sandbox's software GL takes about a tenth of a second a frame, so it cannot show the rate; `moon.mjs` checks that the frames are asked for.)
- **Lit by a sun, and the dark side is the sky.** The sun is fixed in the inertial frame (`MOON.sunAzimuthDeg` 52 from the opening camera's direction, and an elevation that is the real declination, 14 on a debug page), so the moon has the phase its place on the orbit gives it: a gibbous beside the planet. The light on a pixel is `N . sun` through a smoothstep (`litFrom`, `litTo`); the unlit side is the page's colour, so it blends into the page in the light scheme and the dark alike, but the body is SOLID: it hides the stars and the Milky Way behind it (a moon with no light on it is a black spot against the galaxy; on the page's own colour it is invisible). The rim is a band a pixel and a bit wide inside the silhouette: bright where it is lit, and a very faint ring (`MOON.ghostMix`, 13 % of the way from the page's colour to the coastlines') where it is not, so that a moon with no light on it at all can be found and hovered.
- **The Earth's picture treatment, for the ground.** `public/media/moon-grey.png` (256 x 128 grey, 12 steps, baked by `scripts/moon/bake-moon.py`, notice in `moon-grey.NOTICE.txt`) is read by direction like `earth-grey.png` (longitude 0, the near side, in the middle, turned to face the planet); three tones by brightness with the SAME cuts (`SURFACE.cuts`) and tone steps (`SURFACE.tones`, times `MOON.toneBoost` 2.6: a whole planet can be a faint wash, a small body that is a link must show its maria). The Earth's stipple is not used on it: at 13 to 17 cells it read as a screen door, and a dither anchored to the screen would swim across a moving body. The rim is the coastlines' colour taken half the way to the page's (`MOON.outlineMix`).
- **The link.** One `<a class="globe-moon">`, round, invisible, laid over the moon after every tick (so it never lags it), never smaller than 44 px (the moon is small and the target should not be): a tab stop (the card shows with `:focus-visible`, Escape closes it), named by an `aria-label` of the title, the description and the call to action, middle click and "open in a new tab" work. It is not a target (nor a tab stop) where the planet hides most of it, or where it is not in the picture. Touch: the first tap shows the card, the card's own link is the second. It holds the moon still while the card is out (`GlobeRenderer.setMoonHeld`).
- **The box (owner, 2026-10-29).** The moon is boxed like an object in a classification image, but only once it has STAYED: after it has been a target for `DWELL_MS` (1.5 s) without a break the box comes in (`data-seen`, `moon-overlay.ts`); a moon that passes quickly, or goes behind the planet, is never boxed, and the box goes when it stops being a target. It is drawn ON THE ART GRID like the map's own boxes: whole cells of the moon's own grid (`moonBox`: the cells of what is VISIBLE of the disc and `BOX_PAD_CELLS`, 3, of room: where the planet covers part of the moon the box shrinks to fit what is left, `visibleBounds` in `moon.ts`, the box of the moon's disc without the planet's silhouette), one cell thick, the corners solid and the rest of each edge dashed with the area boxes' own `dashingFor` / `edgeLit` (`boxPath`, in cell units, an SVG scaled by the cell with crisp edges), so it steps by a cell as the moon moves. Its colour is a box at rest's, the palette's `peak` level (`GlobeRenderer.moonFrame` writes it, `--moon-box`), and the ink on hover or focus, as a hovered box is. Hollow. The card comes in with the hover and hangs from the box's top-left corner as a place's label does (`cardPlace`): ABOVE the box with the text flush with the box's left edge; BELOW it where there is no room above; always inside the window.
- **The card is a map label's.** No outline: the labels' mono type (a 14 px name over an 11 px muted second line) and, apart from those two lines (14 px of space), an icon-sized arrow at the top right, level with the title: the icon set's `arrow-up-right` (a stem and a right-angled head, square ends, 16 px, stroke 1.5; it was 28 px, centred on the words, until owner, 2026-10-29: a station sign's mark for a link; owner, 2026-10-26, who also took away the "Visit it" line at the bottom right; the words are still in the link's accessible name). It sits on a plate in the page's colour with the label's own feather (`0 0 3px 2px`), so it hides what is behind it (the globe's lines, the ring, the stars) and reads on any of it. The card is as high as the words, its second line on ONE line where the window allows it (the card is up to 26 rem, or the window less 16 px: a phone wraps it); it sits above the box's top-left corner with the gap a label has from its box (3 px). The card is up WITH THE BOX (`data-seen`, after `DWELL_MS`) but unread: its words are SCRAMBLED (`scramble` in `moon-overlay.ts`: random glyphs of the same mono width, spaces kept, so it is the same size and wraps the same, the name muted), as if it were a mystery; held (hover or focus, on the moon or on the card) they come clear from the start over about half a second, the letters not yet clear still shuffling, and stay clear for `READ_MS` (5 s) after it is let go of (hovering again starts the count over), then scramble again, the unknown mark back with them; where the box goes first the card goes with it and is unread the next time (reduced motion: no animation, the same times). The arrow is an UNKNOWN while the words are unread, a blocky question mark of the same weight (`data-glyph`, one svg with two paths), which twitches by a pixel now and then (CSS steps, not under reduced motion), and turns into the arrow half way through the reveal. The real words are the link's accessible name throughout; the card is `aria-hidden`.
- **No anti-aliasing for the Earth (tried, dropped).** `?smooth` tried MSAA, a blur and display-rate frames on the planet; MSAA is on or off, so below 0.5 of intensity nothing visibly changed and above it the Earth was too smooth, and it was removed. What is tuned instead is the idle rotation's speed: `?spin-speed=DEG` (0.01 to 20 degrees a second, default 0.2) on any page, and the frame period follows it (`spinFramePeriod`, `engine/idle-spin.ts`: 100 ms at the default, shorter at a faster one, about half the time the disc takes to move one art pixel). The moon is drawn in whole pixels too (the owner: the speed alone keeps its motion natural).
- **The planet's night (home screen's showcase, world view).** The earth is lit by the moon's sun (`sunDirection`, fixed in the sky), so the planet turns under its light and the terminator stays where it is on the screen. On the dark side the land's ground steps to its lightest, faintest tone and its stipple thins to a fifth, and the graticule, borders, coastlines and the planet's own outline (the horizon ring) go towards the page's colour in three flat grey steps (88 % of the way for the lines, 80 % for the outline; darker in the second round: owner, 2026-10-26, "it just looks a bit dimmer, people could read it as the Americas having less detail") (a one-pixel line thinned in a dither flickers as the planet turns on a phone) (`NIGHT` and `NIGHT_GLSL`, `engine/surface.ts`; `setNight`, `engine/scene.ts`): no blended pixel, and the sea is the page's colour by day and night, so in either scheme the dark side goes towards the page, as the moon's does. **The atmosphere scatters the light a little past the terminator (owner, 2026-10-27):** more than half the planet is lit: the lit side's step is from `NIGHT.litFrom` -0.32 to `litTo` 0.16 (its middle 4.6 degrees past the geometric terminator), and a faint red band lies on the ground either side of that halfway edge (`RIM.twilight`, `twilightMaterial`: redder for the air the light has been through; owner: "it should only tint the existing colours, not the ink-black ocean", so it MULTIPLIES what is under it, and black stays black; dithered like the dark side; the dark page only). **The light page inverts it** (owner, 2026-10-26: the light theme is "turning on the main lights at a party": the sun is so bright that it burns the colour away): there the LIT side is the one that goes towards the page's colour (`washOf` with `flip`, `engine/surface.ts`; `uFlip`, set from the theme's scheme), so the areas nearest the sun are the ones closest to the background, and the dark side has the full tones; a little less than the dark page's wash (`NIGHT.lightGain`, 0.75) so that the lit half is never blank.
